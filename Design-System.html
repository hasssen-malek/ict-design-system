ICT Digital Design System

The design language behind the ICT experience.

This is not a logo sheet. It is the working system that emerged from designing the ICT website — the principles, colour behaviour, type, components, motion and content patterns that make every screen feel like one considered, enterprise-grade product. Apply it and a deck, a proposal, a PDF or a future page will read as part of the same ecosystem.

The work is built for the people who buy and govern enterprise technology — CIOs, CTOs, CEOs, transformation and infrastructure leaders. So the design leads with strategic clarity, not technical noise: structure and typography carry the message; colour is used as hierarchy and rhythm, never decoration; motion is quiet and purposeful.

Premium & restrained

Generous space, hairline structure, deliberate type. Confidence shown through calm, not embellishment.

Enterprise-credible

Built to reassure boards and buyers. Stable, legible, accountable — never trendy, flashy or template-like.

Strategic, not technical

Leads with outcomes and clarity. Technology is the means; business value is the message.

Human & accessible

Plain language, real contrast, considered reading widths. Sophisticated without being cold or sterile.

02 — Visual principles

Ten principles that hold the system together.

Every decision on the site traces back to these. When a new layout is in doubt, return here.

Hierarchy first

One clear focal point per view. Size, weight and space do the ranking — not boxes or colour.

Breathing room

Space is a feature. Sections run 76–150px tall; content is never crowded to the edges.

Controlled density

Say less, with more weight. Trim copy to its proof; resist filler stats and icons.

High, honest contrast

Deep navy or warm paper — committed surfaces, never muddy mid-greys. Text always clears AA.

Colour as rhythm

The palette is distributed across sections so the eye travels. No page leans on one accent.

Structural hairlines

1px rules and divided grids organise content like an editorial layout — calm, exact.

Type carries the design

Space Grotesk display + Inter body. Tight tracking, balanced wrap, generous scale.

Component consistency

The same card, head and button behave identically everywhere. Surprise lives in content, not chrome.

Quiet motion

Soft reveals and small hover shifts. Motion clarifies sequence; it never performs.

Drawn, not stocky

Abstract line-systems over photography clichés. Iconography is single-weight and precise.

03 — Colour system

A dual-surface canvas, an accent palette as rhythm.

Colour is structured in three layers: committed surfaces, a quiet text scale, and a five-hue accent palette distributed across the experience. The accents never fill large areas — they mark, highlight and lead the eye.

Do

  • Give each section one hue; let its tick + keyword share it
  • Vary hue between adjacent sections so a page travels
  • Use bright values on navy, deep values on paper
  • Keep accent area small — marks, keywords, icons

Don't

  • Default everything to blue
  • Fill large blocks or backgrounds with an accent
  • Put a bright (dark-surface) hue as text on paper
  • Use more than one accent inside a single heading

Accessibility — body and UI text always meet WCAG AA (≥4.5:1); large display keywords clear ≥3:1 on their surface. Accent hues are only used at display scale or as graphic marks on the surface they were tuned for.

04 — Gradient & light

Light, not gloss.

The only gradients in the system are large, soft fields of near-black that lift a hero or anchor a CTA. They sit far behind content and are barely perceptible. There are no neon ramps, no coloured glows, no candy gradients.

Hero light field — radial navy, < 8% lift

Hero surface

A quiet rise of light behind the headline.

Do

  • Use one soft radial of near-black per hero
  • Keep transitions wide and low-contrast
  • Vary the light's position per page for subtle distinction

Don't

  • Use saturated or multi-stop colour gradients
  • Add glow, bloom or coloured drop-shadows
  • Let a gradient compete with the content for attention

05 — Typography

Two families. A precise scale.

Space Grotesk is the editorial display voice — used for every heading at weight 500 with tight tracking. Inter carries body and UI. A monospace face handles eyebrows, labels and data. Type, more than colour, is what makes the work feel premium.

Do

  • Set headings at weight 500 with negative tracking
  • Cap measure at ~60ch body / ~20ch display

  • Use mono only for eyebrows, labels and data

Don't

  • Use heavy 700+ weights or all-caps body
  • Stretch lines beyond a comfortable measure
  • Mix in a third display typeface
  • Drop below 15px for any reading text

06 — Layout & grid

An editorial frame, divided by hairlines.

Content lives in a 1320px max container with a fluid gutter. Within it, related items sit in divided grids — cells separated by 1px rules rather than gaps — which gives the work its calm, tabular, editorial structure.

08 — Iconography

One weight. Rounded joins. Honest accent.

Icons are drawn on a 28px grid, single 1.5px stroke, round caps and joins, no fill. They carry brand colour directly — and distribute across a grid rather than all sharing one hue. No badges, plinths or decorative containers.

09 — Illustration

Abstract infrastructure, drawn in line.

Hero and concept art is built from a single language: thin strokes, sparse nodes and one accent-marked focal element, suggesting networks, platforms and flows. It is richer than flat SaaS spot-art but never literal or photographic.

Line-system motif — stroke currentColor, one accent node

Do

  • Build from thin strokes + sparse nodes
  • Mark exactly one focal element in an accent
  • Keep compositions abstract and calm

Don't

  • Use literal 3D, isometric or mascot art
  • Fill shapes with multiple bright colours
  • Add gradients, shadows or texture

10 — Motion & interaction

Calm, purposeful, almost unnoticed.

Motion clarifies sequence and rewards intent — it never performs. One easing curve carries almost everything. Targets the quality bar of Stripe, Linear and Apple: present, never theatrical. All motion respects prefers-reduced-motion.

InteractionBehaviourTiming
Scroll revealopacity 0→1, translateY 22px→0; staggered .08s.8s · (.22,1,.36,1)
Button / card hovercolour + 1–3px lift; arrow nudges 4–5px.25–.35s ease
Text linkunderline wipes in; colour → electric blue.35s · (.22,1,.36,1)
Logo hovergrey lifts toward white · no scale.25s ease
Line-draw motifstroke-dashoffset draws on enter1.6s · (.22,1,.36,1)

Do

  • Use one signature easing for cohesion
  • Animate to clarify sequence and hierarchy
  • Keep hover shifts small (≤5px) and quick

Don't

  • Scale, bounce or spin on hover
  • Loop decorative animation under content
  • Animate so long the user waits on it

11 — Content design

Structured to be scanned, then trusted.

Copy follows a repeatable spine so any section is legible in seconds and credible on a second read.

BlockPattern
Eyebrow2–3 words, category label, uppercase mono
HeadlineOne idea, ≤7 words; highlight a single meaningful word
Supporting copy1–2 sentences, ≤60ch measure, plain enterprise English
Proof points3–6 parallel items; verb-led titles + one-line support
StatisticsDisplay figure + mono label; only when verifiable
Call to actionOne clear next step, action verb, never two competing CTAs

Voice: confident, specific, jargon-light. Lead with the business outcome; let the technology follow. Never pad with filler stats or adjectives.
